Phase 1 — Price Foundation Daily

1
Supplier & quarry prices arrive
Confirmed
Cement from ICL (Adbri-owned) — quoted ~$200/t delivered, invoiced at a different rate. Aggregates and sands from Mawsons quarry — internal source of truth. Admixtures from specialist external suppliers.
Suppliers → Quarry team → Accounts
2
Rates loaded into Command QC & Material Costing workbook
Owner Unknown
Accounts loads rates from supplier invoices into Command. Rod cross-checks uploaded rates against original supplier quotes to confirm invoice values equal quoted values. Ownership split between Rod and Accounts is not fully confirmed.
Accounts + Rod (split unclear) — Command QC, Costing workbook
⚠ Assumed: IT may play a role in loading or automating this step. Not confirmed.
3
Morning exception reports — quote vs. invoice vs. Command
Rod's Knowledge
Rod runs 3 exception reports from Reporting Services each morning. Goes line by line: does this dollar figure match this dollar figure in Command? Also checks truck allocations and volume data. Pure data-alignment work — the strongest single automation candidate in the entire workflow.
Rod — Reporting Services + Command QC

Phase 2 — Quote Build

4
RFQ received and routed
Confirmed
Client request comes in. Coordinators (2 new starters from November, Damian Cherry taking some ownership) facilitate and route to the technical team, quarries, and other relevant parties. Manual process for small fixed-plant and low-volume quotes.
Coordinators → Technical team + Quarry
5
Location defined & mix design produced
Confirmed
Plant selected by proximity to project. Technical team designs the mix — specifying cement (kg/m³), aggregate type and size, sand, SCMs, admixtures. Technical Review Bot is in development but currently manual. The mix code from technical becomes the input to the costing sheet in Step 7.
Technical team — Technical Review Bot (in progress)
6
Plant feasibility check — can this plant actually make the mix?
Rod's Knowledge
Rod informally validates whether the selected plant can physically supply the specified mix. Example: Echuca has 2 silos (fly ash + cement) so can handle SCMs; Colin Ruby has no second silo so cannot. Technical should get this right, but Rod acts as the backstop — from memory alone, no system support.
Rod — informal, no system support
⚠ Key automation opportunity: build a plant capability matrix (boolean rules per plant) to auto-validate mix designs against plant specs and reject or warn before the costing sheet is built.
7
Costing sheet built — recipe × price + cartage + testing + margin
Rod's Knowledge
Coordinators key the mix code from technical into the costing sheet, which pulls the recipe from Command. They then key each ingredient in by hand. Rod builds the pricing: quarry aggregate delivered price, cement (ICL), admixtures. Adds cartage (~$54.85 at 27km — formula unknown), testing ($3/m³ flat), and margin ($281/m³ in the Coleambally sample — typed in, no formula). This sheet uses prices from Command (Step 2), making it wholly dependent on Step 2 being correct.
Coordinators (recipe entry) + Rod (pricing) — Costing workbook + Command QC
7b
Rod's second price check — quality gate before review
Rod's Knowledge
Before the costing sheet goes to management review, Rod performs a second check: does the pricing in the sheet correctly reflect Command? Are there any errors from the coordinator's recipe entry? Acts as a second catch after Step 3's morning exception report.
Rod
8
Costing sheet review — sets margin
Confirmed
Plant manager or regional manager reviews the sheet. Smaller fixed-plant quotes: a "good to go" email. Larger projects: regional or ops manager reviews margin, volume, pricing vs. comparable recent quotes. Mawsons is targeting 20–22% margin to meet Adbri expectations. Notes added at this step.
Plant manager / Regional manager / Ops manager
9
Business sign-off
Confirmed
Regional or ops manager provides final approval. Major projects follow a delegated authority framework including Adbri sign-off. Criteria: margin %, volume, pricing vs. comparable quotes.
Regional / Ops manager → Adbri (major projects only)
10
PDF quote uploaded & sent to client
Confirmed
Coordinators upload the PDF (includes 30-day validity note — currently manual, tracked from memory). Email notification goes to project owner, who sends it to the client. Fixed-plant: plant manager sends. Other projects: regional manager or Steven sends. Currently on P drive; agreed target is SharePoint with automated expiry notifications.
Coordinators (upload) → Project owner (send) — P drive → SharePoint (target)

Phase 3 — Quote to Cash

11
Client accepts
Confirmed
Contract for major projects; a plant-level sale request for smaller work. Lead times are short — builders often book slab pours just 2 weeks out. No formal pipeline of unconfirmed work exists.
Client → Plant or project owner
12
Accepted pricing & recipe entered into Command — project goes live
Owner Unknown
Once the project is awarded, it must be created in Command QC with the correct mix code and pricing so the plant batches to the right recipe and dockets feed correctly into invoicing. Steven believes Rod does this — but not confirmed.
"Rod, I think" — unconfirmed
⚠ Critical gap: if Rod owns this step and leaves end of October, who does it from November? If missed, batching starts with no pricing in Command and every invoice is wrong.
13
Batching — dockets and tickets produced at plant
Confirmed
Plant batches concrete using the mix code from Command. Dockets and tickets are generated per batch and feed directly into the invoicing system.
Plant — Command QC
14
Client invoice checked against dockets & tickets
Confirmed
Accounts checks client invoice against plant dockets and tickets. Rod flags and corrects mismatches in Command. If a supplier has already been paid at the wrong rate, Rod initiates a credit request back through Accounts.
Accounts + Rod — Command QC + Reporting Services
15
Quote expiry & price-increase tracking
Rod's Knowledge
Rod tracks quote expiration dates and upcoming supplier price increases from memory. Example: Hume Link West has a price increase on 1 January. Quotes carry a 30-day validity note — currently just text, no system enforcement. Target: SharePoint list with automated expiry alerts and AI scanning of PDF quotes for key dates.
Rod — memory + local P drive
16
Quarry product code ↔ concrete product code translation
Owner Unknown
Raw quarry codes (e.g. "14mm aggregate, Pyramid Hill") do not align with concrete product codes (e.g. "32MPa 20mm stone 80mm slump"). A manual translation step is required. No shared mapping list exists. There may be a database in Command that could support a comparison, but this has not been confirmed.
Rod or Technical (unclear) — no system support

A — Ownership & Handoffs

A1
Who enters the accepted pricing and recipe into Command to make a project live (Step 12)? If it's Rod, who takes this over from November — and is there any written instruction for how to do it?
If this step is missed at go-live, batching starts with no pricing in Command and every client invoice will be wrong from day one.
P1
A2
When Rod rejects a mix design at Step 6 (plant can't make it), what does the loop-back look like? Who is told, in what format, and what is the expected turnaround time for a redesign?
We need to map the rework path — and to decide whether the automated feasibility check should hard-block the quote or issue a warning and allow override.
P1
A3
Who are the two incoming replacement coordinators, and have they been briefed on Rod's quality checks (Steps 3, 6, 7b)? Is there any written handover material, or does it all need to be extracted before he leaves?
They are the primary day-to-day users of whatever we build. Their understanding of the current process shapes every UX decision.
P1
A4
Which specific steps does Damian Cherry formally own versus informally assist with? Is Damian the single point of continuity between Rod's departure and the new coordinators coming up to speed?
If Damian is the bridge, his knowledge is a second source we can tap before Rod leaves — especially for the undocumented tacit steps.
P1
A5
When a supplier invoice mismatch is caught (Step 3 or 14), who makes the call: pay and seek a credit, dispute the invoice, or reprice the project? Is Rod the decision-maker or does he flag it and someone else decides?
This determines whether the automated alert needs a human approval point or can auto-generate a credit memo request.

B — Pricing Rules & Formulas

B1
Where does the cartage formula come from? The Coleambally file shows $54.85 at 27km — is there a rate card, a freight table, or is this Rod's own calculation? Who will own this after he leaves?
Cartage is a line item on every quote. If there's no formula and only Rod knows the number, the new coordinators have no basis to price it.
P1
B2
The margin in the Coleambally file is $281/m³ across all three mixes — typed in, no formula. Mawsons has a 20–22% Adbri target. How is that applied today? Is it a fixed dollar amount, a % of cost, or Rod's judgment per project?
Understanding the margin rule unlocks the ability to auto-suggest a margin and flag when a quote falls below the Adbri target.
P1
B3
The Material Costing tab in the workbook shows "1st October 2023" for 7 of 8 plants. Is this data current? Who updates it, how often, and what is the process when a supplier puts through a price change?
If the costing tab has stale prices, every quote built on it is wrong from the start — a data quality risk that must be resolved before automation.
P1
B4
What does the "standard mix from list price" process look like? Is it a completely separate, simpler flow — or the same costing sheet with fewer steps? How many quotes per week are standard vs. bespoke?
If standard mixes are the majority by volume, they may deserve their own simplified flow in the tool rather than a cut-down version of the full process.
P1
B5
The costing workbook contains notes like "Current + $6.80t", "Bulk + 15%", and "plus 50%". What do these adjustments represent — fuel levies, freight surcharges, seasonal adjustments? Who set them and when?
Pricing rules stored as free text won't survive automation — they need to be extracted and formalised as explicit rules.
P2

C — Data & Systems

C1
What are the exact three exception reports Rod runs each morning? What fields are compared in each, what does a "pass" look like, and what does a "fail" trigger? Can we get a sample output from Reporting Services?
These reports are the single clearest automation target. We cannot replicate them without knowing exactly what is being compared and what the output looks like.
P1
C2
API access to Command QC — has Daryl formally approved this? What is the authentication method, and are there any data governance or security requirements we need to meet before we can start pulling data?
The entire automation stack depends on this access. A delay here is a project delay of equivalent length.
P1
C3
Is there an existing mapping table anywhere between quarry product codes (e.g. "14mm aggregate, Pyramid Hill") and concrete product codes (e.g. "32MPa 20mm stone 80mm slump")? Or is this translation entirely in Rod's head?
If no mapping exists, it needs to be built with Rod before he leaves. The two replacement coordinators will not know this.
P1
C4
Is there any existing documentation of which plants stock which aggregates, which SCM silos they have, and their capacity constraints? Or does the plant capability matrix need to be built from scratch through Rod and the plant managers?
This matrix is the foundation for automating the plant feasibility check (Step 6) — it cannot be reconstructed after Rod leaves.
P1
C5
What format does the technical team use to deliver a mix design today — an email, a document, a mix code from Command? Is the recipe already in Command when it reaches the coordinator, or does the coordinator transcribe it from a document?
The handover between Steps 5 and 7 is where transcription errors enter the costing sheet. Knowing the format tells us where to intercept and automate the data transfer.
P1

D — Automation Scope & Boundaries

D1
After automation, which decisions must stay with a human? Our working assumption: margin sign-off (Steps 8–9), plant feasibility override (Step 6), and supplier dispute resolution (Step 14) remain human-in-the-loop. Do you agree — and are there others?
This shapes the entire system architecture: alert-and-wait, auto-resolve, or hard-block.
P2
D2
For quote expiry notifications (Step 15): who receives the alert, how far in advance (30 days? 7 days? both?), and what action should the notification prompt — renew, reprice, or escalate?
Notifications without a clear call-to-action get ignored. The intended action defines what the alert must contain and who it goes to.
P2
D3
Who are the primary day-to-day users of the tool — the two new coordinators, Damian, Rod in January, or all three? What devices and environments do they work in (desktop at a desk, tablet at the plant, phone on the road)?
User experience decisions depend entirely on who the users are and the context they're working in.

R4 — Coleambally Costing Workbook

Coleambally Concrete Evidence — Darlington Point Sub (1 Oct 2026)

Excel file · 3 mix designs · ~1,700 m³ total · Used as a sample by Globant for discovery analysis

  • Mix structure: Three concrete mixes priced in a single workbook — each row shows ingredients (cement, aggregate, sand, admixtures) with kg/m³ quantities and delivered unit prices.
  • Margin: $281/m³ applied identically across all three mixes — typed in, no formula. Steven confirmed there is no fixed rule; margin varies by project and he sees opportunity to switch to a % target.
  • Cartage: $54.85 at 27km — formula unknown. Steven does not know where it comes from.
  • Testing: $3/m³ flat allowance. Changes to a project-specific figure for major projects.
  • Material Costing tab: Dated "1st October 2023" for 7 of 8 plants. Contains adjustments like "Current + $6.80t", "Bulk + 15%", "plus 50%" — likely fuel levies or freight surcharges; origin and update process unknown.
  • Recipe source: Mix code supplied by technical team → coordinators key it into the costing sheet by hand from Command. Manual transcription step.
  • Cement supplier: ICL only (Adbri-owned). No other cement suppliers used.
